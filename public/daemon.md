[ABOUT]
Twenty-plus years in security. Principal security engineer working where AI meets offensive security. BJJ black belt. Philosophy autodidact.

Security and philosophy scratch the same itch: deconstructing black boxes to understand how they actually work. Security is applied epistemology. The question is never "did the scanner pass" but "how do we *know* this system is secure," and most of what I build is machinery for answering that honestly.

The pursuit itself is the purpose.

[MISSION]
Encode tacit security expertise into systems that scale beyond any individual: agents that eliminate whole vulnerability classes instead of finding one bug at a time, and the verification machinery that makes their output trustworthy without a human re-checking it by hand.

Under that, the older aim: dismantle checkbox security and fear-based risk assessment, and put data-driven decisions and AI-augmented human judgment in their place.

Define the hacker-strategist by doing the work. Technical depth combined with strategic thinking, adversarial creativity with systems understanding. Demonstrate where the role lives and what it produces.

[EXPLORATIONS]
Current areas of active investigation and learning.

- Intent Decomposition: A dedicated agent step between what you ask for and what gets built, because the gap is in the words you never said.
- Client-Informed Hunting: Reading the policy a browser ships and testing whether the server agrees, with no source access and no signature matching.
- Verification Without Trust: Pipelines where no agent grades its own work, every claim is replayed in an isolated environment, and a laundered non-answer counts as worse than a crash.
- Doctrine for Agents: A wikilinked corpus of what has been built with AI and what happened, authored so an agent at design time can ask what kind of problem it has.
- Claim-First Documents: Technical writing where every figure states what it proves and every number carries provenance, compiled from plain markdown.
